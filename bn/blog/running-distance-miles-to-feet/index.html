How to Convert Running Distance: Miles to Feet

Published on July 13, 2026 • 5 min read

For runners, hikers, and walkers, measuring workouts is usually done in miles, kilometers, or track laps. However, there are times when knowing your total distance in feet is incredibly satisfying or necessary for training breakdowns.

Whether you're calculating track laps or sizing up standard road race lengths, here is how popular running distances translate to total feet.

1. The Standard 400m Running Track

Most school and competition running tracks are 400 meters long in lane 1. In imperial units, this standard lap is approximately 1,312 feet.

To run a full mile, you must complete slightly more than 4 laps:
4 laps × 1,320 feet = 5,280 feet (exactly 1 mile).

2. The 5K Race (5 Kilometers)

The 5K is one of the most popular road race distances in the world. 5 kilometers is equivalent to approximately 3.10686 miles.

Using our standard conversion rate:
3.10686 mi × 5,280 ft = 16,404.22 feet.

For most general references, runners round the 5K running distance to 16,400 feet.

3. The 10K Race (10 Kilometers)

Double the 5K, a 10K race is approximately 6.21371 miles.
In feet:
6.21371 mi × 5,280 ft = 32,808.39 feet (commonly rounded to 32,800 feet).

4. The Half Marathon (13.1 Miles)

A half marathon is exactly 13.1094 miles.
In feet:
13.1094 mi × 5,280 ft = 69,217.63 feet (approx. 69,200 feet).

5. The Full Marathon (26.2 Miles)

The historic marathon distance is officially 26 miles and 385 yards, which translates to exactly 26.21875 miles.

Multiplying this by our unit scale:
26.21875 mi × 5,280 ft = 138,435 feet.
